Course Curriculum

Month 1

The Actor's Instrument

Week 1: The Body – The Actor's First Instrument

To help students understand their body as the primary tool of performance and develop awareness, control, flexibility, and expressiveness.

Understanding and Controlling the Body and Physical Expression

Discussion: Actor's instrument, body awareness, emotions manifest, everyday movement and stage movement,Tension vs Relaxation,Physical habits and mannerisms,Body communication.

Practical Work: warm-up, Body scanning exercises, posture, Alignment and balance exercises, Walking in space, Isolation , Tempo and Leading-center , rhythm exercises , Physical storytelling .

Week 2: Voice and Speech – The Actor's Audible Instrument

To develop awareness of breath, voice production, articulation, and vocal expression while reinforcing the physical awareness developed in Week 1.

Revision and Introduction to Voice , Speech and Vocal Expression

Revision : Body warm-up and isolation ,Alignment and posture , Walking in space , Silent character studies

Discussion: Important of Voice , Relationship between body, breath, and voice. , Difference between speaking and performing speech. , Common vocal habits and limitations, Clarity versus loudness , Articulation and diction , The importance of language in performance

Practical Work: Breath support and awareness exercises , Diaphragmatic breathing, Humming and resonance exercises, Vocal warm-ups, Jaw, tongue, lip, Projection exercises, Tongue twisters, Resonance work, Reading prose and poetry aloud

Week 3: Introduction to Improvisation – Responding in the Moment.

To help students apply body and voice techniques learned in previous weeks while developing spontaneity, imagination, listening skills, and stage co1nfidence.

Revision : Body awareness and movement exercises , Alignment and posture , Breath control and voice warm-ups , Articulation and projection exercises

Discussion: What is improvisation? , Why is improvisation important for actors? , The difference between planning and responding , Trust, acceptance, and presence on stage.

Practical Work: "Yes, And..." exercises, Word association games, Status and relationship exercises, Movement-based improvisations, Vocal improvisations using sounds and emotions

Week 4: Advanced Improvisation – Creating Character and Story

To strengthen improvisational skills by introducing character, conflict, relationships, and storytelling through body and voice.

Revision : Body movement exercises, Voice and speech exercises, Basic improvisation principles, Listening and responding exercises

Discussion: Creating character through physicality, Creating character through voice, Understanding objectives and conflict, Storytelling without a script.

Practical Work: Character walks, Voice transformation exercises, Improvised scenes with given circumstances, Emotion and status improvisations, Character-to-character interactions

Month 2

Week 5: Introduction to Characterization – Building Character Through the Body

To introduce students to the process of character creation and help them understand how physical choices influence characterization.

Revision : Body awareness and movement exercises, Voice and speech warm-ups, Improvisation exercises, Character creation through spontaneous play

Discussion: What is a character?, Difference between self and character, How physicality shapes personality, Observing human behavior as a source for character creation.

Practical Work: Character walks, Centre-led movement (head, chest, hips, knees, feet), Age transformation exercises, Occupation-based physicality, Animal-to-human character transformation

Week 6: Characterization Through Energy and Presence

To explore how energy, rhythm, tempo, and inner life contribute to the creation of believable characters.

Revision : Character walks, Physical transformation exercises, Improvisation and storytelling techniques

Discussion: What is character energy?, External versus internal characterization, How energy affects movement, speech, and behavior, Understanding a character's emotional and psychological state.

Practical Work: Energy scale exercises (1–10), Tempo-rhythm exploration, Status and power dynamics, Emotional energy exercises, Character voice exploration

Improvisation Work Characters in everyday situations, Characters under pressure, Relationship-based improvisations

Group ExercisesEnsemble scenes using contrasting energies,Character interactions without scripted dialogue

Week 7: The Actor's Tools – Observation and Awareness

To introduce students to observation as a primary acting tool and develop their ability to gather material from real life for character creation and performance.

Revision : Body and movement exercises, Voice and speech warm-ups, Characterization exercises, Energy and presence work

Discussion: What are an actor's tools?, Why is observation important?, Learning from everyday life, The actor as an observer of human behaviour.

Practical Work: Observation exercises, Recreating observed behaviour, Gesture and habit studies, Mimicry versus truthful observation, Physical and vocal detail exercises

Week 8: The Actor's Imagination – Creating Life Beyond Reality

To help students develop imagination as a creative acting tool and learn how to create believable circumstances, relationships, and worlds on stage.

Revision : Observation exercises, Characterization work, Improvisation techniques, Body and voice integration

Discussion: What is imagination in acting?, Reality versus theatrical reality, The power of "What If?", Belief and commitment in performance.

Practical Work: Character walks, Centre-led movement (head, chest, hips, knees, feet), Age transformation exercises, Occupation-based physicality, Animal-to-human character transformation

Month 3

Scene Work and Performance Application

To provide students with the opportunity to apply all the techniques learned throughout the course in scripted and devised scenes. Students will explore character, relationships, objectives, imagination, body, voice, and improvisation within a performance context.
